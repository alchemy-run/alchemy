Everything on PlanetScale belongs to an **organization**: its databases and their branches, its members, and its bill. There are two kinds of access to keep apart. A deploy credential lets Alchemy create and change databases. A **database credential** lets your app connect to one branch.

## Sign in with a profile

Alchemy reaches an organization through a [profile](/environments/profiles):

```sh
alchemy profile edit --add Planetscale
```

**OAuth** signs you in through the browser, and Alchemy refreshes the session for you. Or store a **service token**: a token that belongs to the organization, not to a person, carrying the permissions you give it. Alchemy deploys into the organization in your profile: the one your service token belongs to, or the one you pick when you sign in with OAuth. See [Setup](/planetscale/setup).

## One profile per organization

A [stage](/environments/stages) decides what you deploy. A profile decides where. If production lives in its own organization, give it its own profile:

```sh
alchemy profile create prod
alchemy profile edit --profile prod --add Planetscale
alchemy deploy --stage prod --profile prod
```

Within one organization, stages get their own branches, so a pull request never touches production's data.

## Database credentials

Your app never holds the deploy token. It connects with credentials that reach one branch, and only as far as their role allows: a Postgres **role** inherits Postgres's own roles, and a MySQL **password** carries a role like `readwriter`. Give a reporting job a read-only credential and the API a read-write one. See [Credentials](/planetscale/data/credentials).

## In CI

With `CI=true`, Alchemy skips the profile and reads `PLANETSCALE_API_TOKEN_ID`, `PLANETSCALE_API_TOKEN` and `PLANETSCALE_ORGANIZATION` from the environment. Use a service token there, so it outlives anyone's login. See [CI](/environments/ci).
