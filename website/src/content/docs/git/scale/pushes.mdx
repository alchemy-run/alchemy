---

A `git push` sends one pack. The Worker receives it as a stream and
never holds it whole:

1. The first 1 MiB is read for the ref commands, and
   [Auth](/git/blocks/auth) rules on them before any pack byte is
   inspected.
2. The body streams through in 8 MiB parts. Each part goes to the
   [hasher](/git/blocks/hasher) as it lands and is parked in the
   [blob store](/git/blocks/blob-store) as a multipart upload.
3. Hashed objects reach the repository's Durable Object as batches of
   rows over RPC: coordinates into the parked pack for large objects,
   inline bytes for small ones.
4. The Durable Object checks connectivity over the staged rows,
   updates the commit graph, moves the refs in one transaction, and
   schedules compaction.

The pack never enters the Durable Object. Its memory, CPU, and egress
are untouched by push size, and its share of a push is SQL.

## Memory

The Worker retains a 16 MiB window of the body for objects that span
parts. Peak memory is that window plus the parts in flight, whatever
the push size. Pushes share a 64 MiB budget per isolate, and one that
does not fit waits for an earlier one to drain.

## Hashing

Each 4 MiB chunk is hashed where the hasher Layer runs. Inline, that
is the receiving thread, one chunk after another. On loaded Workers,
four chunks at once. On Lambda, every chunk at once. An object that
straddles two chunks is hashed from its own header the moment its
first chunk settles, so a 10 MB blob spanning three parts does not
wait for the last one.

Deltas whose base is in another chunk are resolved as a second round
of hasher jobs, and the resolved blobs are written out as a pack of
their own so a fetch never re-resolves them.

## Measured

One client, a fresh repository on each host, the same edge. The
delta-heavy pack is 40 MiB and 15.6k objects, the whole-blob pack is
44 MiB:

| | 40 MiB deltas | 44 MiB blobs | incremental |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| GitHub | 3.3 s | 4.6 s | 1.2 s |
| `HasherInline` | 9.9–13.5 s | 8.8 s | 0.2–0.4 s |
| `HasherWorkerLoader` | 5.9 s best, 6.5 s median | 4.8 s best, 6.4 s median | 0.2–0.4 s |
| `HasherLambda` | 6.8–7.9 s | 6.3–6.7 s | 0.3–0.8 s |

Server-side, the route's own time on the loader hasher is 3.4–3.9 s
for the delta-heavy push and 2.2–3.6 s for the whole-blob push. The
spread in the table is the client's uplink. What remains on the
delta-heavy push is the hashing waves, ten chunks over four isolates,
and the delta rounds behind them.

The largest real push measured is the alchemy monorepo: 44,051 objects
in a 67 MiB thin pack, cloned back byte-identical under
`git fsck --strict`.

## What a push reports

`lastPush` on the repository splits the last push into ingest,
staging, connectivity, and finalize. When a push is slow, that split
says whether the time went to hashing, to the round trips into the
Durable Object, or to the commit.
